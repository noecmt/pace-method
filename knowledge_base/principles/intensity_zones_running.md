The 5-zone pace model for running, anchored on **threshold pace** (the pace an athlete can sustain for ~60 minutes at maximal effort — equivalent to T-pace in the Daniels system and lactate threshold pace in most lab protocols). Zones are expressed as a factor of `threshold_pace_sec_km`; a factor > 1 means slower than threshold (easier), < 1 means faster (harder).

- **Zone 1** (Easy/Recovery, factor 1.15–1.40×): fully conversational, low physiological stress; base volume, recovery jogs, warm-up/cool-down
- **Zone 2** (Aerobic, factor 1.08–1.15×): steady aerobic pace, able to speak in sentences; core of weekly mileage, long runs
- **Zone 3** (Tempo, factor 1.02–1.08×): comfortably hard, short sentences only; raises the lactate threshold, the base-phase sweet-spot equivalent for running
- **Zone 4** (Threshold, factor 0.97–1.02×): hard sustained effort, barely speaking; interval work at or just below the lactate threshold (T-pace repeats)
- **Zone 5** (Interval, factor 0.85–0.97×): very hard, unsustainable beyond 3-5 min; VO2max intervals and repetitions for economy and speed

Most of an endurance runner's weekly volume is done in Zone 1-2. Zone 5 is reserved for short, high-quality interval blocks in the build phase.
